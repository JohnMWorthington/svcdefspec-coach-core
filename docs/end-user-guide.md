# Service Definition & Specification Coach — CORE User Guide

**Controlled Release | v0.1.28 | 19 November 2026**

This guide helps you choose a focused exercise or a full service-definition engagement. Both use the **same CORE method** and its outside-in reasoning, evidence discipline, Current/Future/Open distinctions and human approval safeguards. The Controlled Release invites practical field use and feedback; it does not claim certification or an audit.

## Quick start

1. Download `core/svcdefspec-core.md` from this official repository.
2. Start a fresh ChatGPT or Claude conversation and upload the CORE method plus service evidence that you are permitted to share. Evidence may include a service agreement, catalog, spreadsheet, reports, notes or a plain-language description.
3. Choose one of the starter prompts below. Ordinary language is enough.
4. Review all material outputs before using them in organizational or contractual decisions.

## Focused exercises versus full-method coaching

| | Focused exercise | Full-method coaching |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | One bounded question or activity | Define/reconstruct a complete service relationship |
| Example | Clarify a service boundary, examine one customer moment, or review one agreement concern | Build the Current model, examine representative interactions, then align the agreement |
| Output | Requested result only; synchronized Map and Canvas when a modeling cycle occurs | One evolving canonical model, Map and Canvas |
| Stop point | Stop at the exercise boundary | Seek authorization before moving between phases |

**Reusable preamble:** Use the uploaded Service Definition & Specification Coach CORE method. Work outside-in. Separate evidence, inference, conflict and Open items; distinguish Current from Future; respect human decision-making and phase gates.

## Five starter prompts, with short examples

### A. Focused: understand the boundary
> Help me determine what service relationship this material describes. Identify the likely Customer, Provider, recurring situation and Useful Completion. Keep conclusions provisional where evidence is missing. Stop after this exercise.

**Example:** HR calls onboarding, badge activation and laptop provisioning three services. Explore which may be customer-facing services, variants or supporting elements without deciding the answer in advance.

### B. Focused: one customer moment
> Help me examine one important customer moment in this service: [describe the situation]. Show the Customer Job, Value Expectations and available experience evidence. Do not design workflows or move to agreement drafting.

**Example:** A new employee arrives but their badge does not open the door. Explore what they were trying to accomplish, what they expected and what is known about the event.

### C. Focused: an agreement concern
> Examine this clause or concern: [paste or describe]. If the service model is not sufficiently understood, identify the Current-state and boundary context necessary to interpret it. Do not assume a missing section is a defect or draft new commitments.

**Example:** Equipment delivery is promised within two days, but the employee cannot begin work. Identify what the clause establishes and what remains Open.

### D. Full method: Existing Service
> Review these materials and help me understand the service as it exists today. Start with See it + Specify it. Show a synchronized Service Model Map and USM Service Model Canvas, preserve Open items, and ask only material questions. Do not recommend changes yet.

**Example:** Upload the onboarding agreement, checklist, user feedback and relevant evidence; reconstruct the Current service before considering changes.

### E. Full method: New Service
> Help me define a proposed new service from the Customer's recurring situation and Useful Completion. Mark proposed elements as Future, and proceed one phase at a time.

**Example:** A local authority proposes an online permit service. Start with an applicant's situation and the useful result, treating arrangements as proposed until authorized.

## What happens in the full method

**Phase 1 — See it + Specify it.** Establish Customer, Provider, recurring situation, Useful Completion, material Moments of Truth, Facility and Support, Functionality and Functioning. Develop the Map and Canvas from the same model. Distinguish evidence, inference, conflict and Open items. Do not recommend improvements during this phase.

**Phase 2 — Operate it.** After the user agrees to proceed, examine a representative set of customer-to-provider interactions: Wish/Agree, Change Request/Change, Failure Recovery/Recover and Service Request/Operate as justified. Connect each to customer-relevant results, high-level Provider responsibilities and material dependencies. Do not build detailed workflows, routes, approvals, RACI or procedures.

**Phase 3 — Agree it.** When authorized, begin with a USM Alignment & Gap Review. Distinguish service-definition gaps, agreement-documentation gaps and genuine Future design decisions. Do not silently turn assumptions or proposals into commitments.

## Service Model Map and editable USM Canvas

The Service Model Map runs left-to-right from **Recurring Situation → Moments of Truth → Useful Completion**. The Canvas is its synchronized structured specification companion. Both derive from one canonical model, not from independent interpretations.

The official Excel-based **USM Service Model Canvas** is available through the [USM Portal](https://usm-portal.com/usm-service-model-canvas-2/?lang=en) to registered USM users. When using the approved template, preserve its English single-sheet USM structure, headings, merged cells and generic-before-specific service blocks. Describe Facility and Support separately, including each one's Functionality and Functioning. Preserve unknowns as Open, rather than inventing targets, reports or meetings. The Canvas is not the Service Agreement.

**Example:** In an onboarding service, workplace, approved access and equipment may be Facility elements (depending on the agreed boundary). Assistance with a failed sign-in is Support. What each can do is Functionality; how well it performs is Functioning.

If the environment can populate editable Excel files, verify the finished workbook. Otherwise use a synchronized on-screen Canvas; file automation is not required to use CORE. Respect the official template's redistribution terms.

## Quality and limitations

Look for a coherent customer-facing boundary, useful completion, evidence-aware assumptions, few material questions and visible Open items. Teams, tools, suppliers, catalog entries and platform components do not automatically become separate services. Neither a closed ticket nor supplier uptime alone proves that the Customer can resume useful work.

The Coach is a facilitation aid. It is not a maturity assessment, an audit, a certification, a formal Dual Lens diagnosis, legal or commercial authority, or a substitute for accountable human decisions. Its normal endpoint is a human-reviewed canonical service model and gap-aware Service Agreement foundation; detailed workflow/process implementation follows separately.
